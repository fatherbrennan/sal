**My recommendation is to compare spending power, financial reward, personal package value,
and time commitment together.** A single total can conceal an unaffordable cash position,
restricted retirement savings, subjective perks, or excessive hours.

The closest names for the user's examples are:
- Net package value after cash costs: after-tax cash + net super + personal perks - cash job costs.
- Net package value after costs and travel time: the previous amount - valued commute time.
- After-tax cash plus net super per standard paid hour.
- Net package value after cash costs per work-and-travel hour.

"All expenses" is too broad for the second measure. It includes specified job costs and a
personal valuation of travel time; it does not include every household expense. "True expenses"
is also ambiguous between cash outgoings and economic costs such as depreciation. State the
selected cost basis explicitly.

**The proposed priority order is a review and presentation recommendation, not a universal
ranking of importance or an implementation sequence.** Dependencies must be correct before
their totals can be used. A high-value metric can remain unavailable until its inputs and rules
are supported. A low display priority does not authorise removal of an existing required view.

The order assumes an ordinary comparison of alternative employee offers over a normal full year.
It is not an assumption about this user's circumstances. Relevant household, contractor, leave,
liquidity or contractual issues can promote a conditional metric to the first group.

**Common accounting definitions apply to the candidate formulas.** These are proposed comparison
definitions, not assertions that every current source result already implements the full ledger.

| Symbol | Meaning                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| G      | Offer cash remuneration before valid super redirection, personal tax and payroll deductions; excludes employer super, non-cash perks and separately classified reimbursements.            |
| Se     | Employer-funded super from the offer, excluding salary sacrificed from G.                                                                                                                 |
| C      | Annual offer-attributable cash after supported cash-funded assessed taxes, levies, HELP and matched earnings redirected to super; before job costs and discretionary non-super transfers. |
| S      | Matched net super from Se and earnings redirected from this offer, after supported fund-funded taxes/adjustments.                                                                         |
| P      | Personally useful additive perk value, excluding expense savings already represented elsewhere.                                                                                           |
| E      | Incremental unreimbursed cash job and commute costs for the same scenario and period, counted once.                                                                                       |
| Hp     | Standard annual paid hours under the explicit comparison conversion.                                                                                                                      |
| Hw     | Annual hours worked or explicitly estimated to be worked, from a complete relevant work pattern.                                                                                          |
| Ht     | Annual commuting hours.                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Hu     | Additional non-overlapping constrained time, if explicitly included in an extended view.                                                                                                  |
| V      | Monetised commute time under the existing net convention: (C / Hp) x Ht, subject to the cash-basis reconciliation below.                                                                  |
| D      | Supported debt reduction attributable to the offer, used only in the separately labelled accounting view A10.                                                                             |
| Delta  | Alternative offer result minus the selected baseline result, using the same named measure.                                                                                                |

Gross remuneration is G + Se. Gross package at personal value is G + Se + P.
The matching gross commute-time valuation is ((G + Se + P) / Hp) x Ht.

The project uses incremental annual attribution: supported annual liabilities with the offer
minus liabilities without it. Both scenarios require valid inputs and recomputed eligibility,
deductions and linked income tests. This is a comparison convention, not a statutory assignment
of one personal tax bill to an employer. A payer's tax-free-threshold declaration affects
withholding; it does not create another annual tax-free threshold.

C deliberately excludes ordinary spending and transfers unrelated to the job. If payroll
already paid a job expense, a "before job costs" C requires that expense to be identified and
restored before E removes it once. If a deduction was a savings transfer, restoring it changes
the presentation of the destination, not the income received.

For contractors, a net-cash starting point can already include operating costs. E must then
exclude those same costs. Alternatively use a clearly defined pre-cost starting point and
subtract them once. These alternatives must not be mixed in one comparison.

S includes offer-funded voluntary contributions only where the reduction in C is matched.
Moving existing external savings into super is not extra value created by this offer.
Employer contributions, salary sacrifice and after-tax personal contributions remain visible.

If a personal tax liability is funded from super, it reduces S rather than also reducing C.
Unknown funding allocation or fund treatment can block separate cash/super amounts even when
some independently established aggregate remains available. Do not substitute zero.

The current code's cash numerator and the broader C proposed here need reconciliation before
reuse. The source does not establish a complete annual classification of every discretionary
payroll destination. The existing approved time-rate basis must not be silently changed to
the restored full-worth C if those amounts differ. This is a definition issue for the reviewer,
not permission to modify the time convention.

Gross base cash, gross package including perks, net super, costs and tax per paid hour can remain
component breakdowns. They do not each need a separate headline score. Annual, monthly and
weekly averages are display conversions, not independent measures of worth or payroll forecasts.

Additional constrained time can include mandatory preparation, unpaid administration, required
travel beyond commuting, or genuinely constrained unpaid breaks. Work already counted in Hw
cannot also enter Hu. On-call availability should retain its own classification; an hour of
availability must not silently become an hour of active work.

Unknown or non-positive hour denominators cannot produce a valid rate. Commute-time valuation
also needs a meaningful rate; unknown or negative cash must not quietly become a zero or
negative commute burden. Paid-time conversion is not attendance, and leave entitlement does not
automatically reduce Hw or create fewer journeys.

Keep monetary time deductions separate from the work-and-travel denominator. Using both in
one headline metric adjusts for the same travel twice. If deliberately proposed later, it needs
a separate explanation and approval. The existing
[hourly boundary](research/ato/remuneration-reconciliation.md) preserves this separation.

Use the incremental cost caused by the offer relative to an explicit consistent personal baseline.
For example, an extra lunch cost is not automatically the entire restaurant bill if another
meal would have been bought anyway. Do not invent standard costs.

Perk/expense overlap is particularly important: free parking cannot improve the comparison both
through lower E and a second equal perk value. Either normalise the cost ledger consistently
and represent the benefit once, or exclude the already represented saving from P. A generic
perk amount alone does not establish this link.

Entered vehicle costs currently include running costs and trip charges, excluding allocated
insurance, registration and depreciation. C8 is broader research, not a reversal of that
decision. A job requiring an otherwise unnecessary vehicle may justify a separately evidenced
incremental ownership scenario, subject to approval.

The ATO kilometre-rate alternative is a broader cost estimate, not proof of actual cash spending,
a running-cost-only amount, or deductible commuting. Never combine it with the running costs it
already represents. See [cost calculations](src/lib/calculations/costs.ts) and
[comparison fields](research/comparison-fields.md).

D7-D9 require directional interpretation. Equal hours make an incremental-hour ratio undefined.
More value with fewer hours is a dominance result, not a negative hourly wage. More hours with
less value is an adverse trade-off. The chosen value numerator must stay visible.

D10-D12 require full scenario recalculation, including relevant super, taxes, eligibility,
costs and time-rate changes. Do not multiply a gross difference by one marginal tax rate.
Thresholds and discontinuities can mean no feasible result or more than one crossing; a solver
must not assume a unique solution.

Keep personal tax context consistent across alternatives. If an override changes the household
or other personal assumptions, identify the result as a scenario difference rather than
attributing the entire difference to the job.

**Material gaps and double-counting risks determine whether a candidate is trustworthy.**
- Missing is not zero. Unknown tax or super can block a net total while independent gross,
  expense or time measures remain visible.
- All components need the same financial year, scenario, recurrence and inclusion basis.
  Guaranteed, target and one-off values cannot be mixed silently.
- Tax estimates remain labelled estimates. A numerical value does not prove final assessment
  precision or complete special-case treatment.
- Expense deductibility is independent of economic cost. A deductible expense is still spent;
  its supported tax effect belongs inside recalculated C, not a second added tax saving.
- Reimbursement and expense must be linked once. An allowance is not automatically reimbursement.
  Direct employer payment and reimbursed employee payment require consistent classification.
- Perk personal value does not establish tax exemption, taxable benefit value or a reportable
  amount. Additional employer super and cash allowances belong in their own components.
- A perk already represented by reduced E cannot also add the same saving through P.
- Contributions from existing savings are not employer reward. Voluntary transfers must reduce
  one destination when increasing another.
- Net contributions are not actual fund receipt or account growth. Timing, fund tax, fees,
  insurance, returns and special super rules require separate facts where applicable.
- Paid hours, worked hours, billable hours, constrained availability and travel hours are distinct.
  No duration may enter two categories unless the overlap is expressly intended and explained.
- Salary-derived time valuation is a personal comparison convention, not a cash expense, tax
  deduction, proof of alternative paid work, or universal market price of leisure.
- Standard annual averages do not establish payment dates, actual annual bank deposits or
  assessment credits. Do not subtract annual liability and withholding twice.
- Existing shared income can change the incremental tax result without becoming offer cash.
  A partner's own liability changes belong to a separately defined household view.
- Base-only recalculation needs valid counterfactual reporting and family inputs. Removing an
  earnings amount alone may not establish the correct base-only tax scenario.
- Lower tax or fewer hours is not automatically a better overall offer. Colour/direction rules
  need the metric's meaning; neutral descriptors should not imply a universal preference.
- A dominant result on selected numerical dimensions says nothing definitive about Q factors.
- Percentage differences require a meaningful baseline; zero/negative baselines should not
  produce misleading percentages.
- Bounds and sensitivities require explicit ranges. Do not invent probabilities, investment
  returns, inflation, promotion rates, time values, job-loss rates or utilisation.
- No existing storage reset, schema relaxation, dependency addition or runtime network service
  is implied by this research. Existing data and unknowns must survive any later authorised work.

The broader missing inputs include payroll-deduction destinations; actual payment/credit dates;
complete fund-tax and receipt information; expense/perk replacement links; complete additional
work and constrained-time records; household assistance rules; contractor receivables/payables;
billable utilisation; transition dates; equity terms; and explicit future assumptions.
