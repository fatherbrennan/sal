# Employment, benefits, and cost evidence

Verified on **2026-10-22 UTC** by retrieving the cited primary page and inspecting the relevant body text. These are local paraphrased evidence notes, not complete copies of the remote pages. Search snippets alone were not accepted as verification.

Each entry records the verified dependency and its limits. Unless stated, the page's publication/update date was not established; the verification date is not an enactment date. Recheck the source and applicable instrument before implementing a dated entitlement. National-system guidance must not be applied automatically to a state-system worker.

## E01 — Employment-system coverage

Source: [Fair Work Ombudsman: National Employment Standards](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/employment-conditions/national-employment-standards), sections “Minimum entitlements” and “Who the NES covers”.

Verified: the NES covers employees in the national workplace relations system. Awards, agreements, and contracts cannot exclude or reduce its minimum conditions. Casual employees have only some NES entitlements; the page includes paid family and domestic violence leave among them.

Limit: this does not identify which system covers an arbitrary employer or determine an individual's entitlements. Capture jurisdiction and the actual governing instrument.

## E02 — Contract inputs

Source: [business.gov.au: Create an employment contract](https://business.gov.au/people/employees/create-an-employment-contract), “What to include in an employment contract”.

Verified: employment contracts identify the parties, role, employment type and dates, work hours, pay and allowances, leave, and notice requirements. Different arrangements require different contract treatment.

Limit: an offer's actual terms must come from that offer; this source supplies no salary, benefits, roster, or probabilities for sal users.

## E03 — Salary packaging

Source: [ASIC Moneysmart: Salary packaging](https://moneysmart.gov.au/work-and-tax/salary-packaging), “How salary packaging works” and “What you can salary package”. Page updated **12 October 2026**.

Verified: salary packaging combines cash income with benefits. Its value depends on income, offered benefits, and associated costs. The page distinguishes fringe benefits, exempt benefits, and superannuation, and identifies employer-specific offerings and concessions.

Limit: use the [ATO references](../ato/README.md) for tax classifications, conditions, and effective dates. Do not turn an employer's benefit cost into the worker's personal value automatically. Future changes mentioned on this page need enacted-rule verification.

## E04 — Annualised wages and included entitlements

Source: [Fair Work Ombudsman: Annualised wages and salaries](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/pay-and-wages/minimum-wages/annualised-wages-and-salaries), “Annualised wage arrangements” and “Awards with annualised wage arrangements”.

Verified: an award annualised-wage arrangement differs from a salary under a contract. Depending on the instrument, the annualised amount can cover minimum pay, penalties, overtime, allowances, and leave loading; some awards impose additional-payment limits.

Limit: record which components are already included. A salary figure does not establish that every extra payment is included or that the arrangement is legally sufficient.

## E05 — Hours and rosters

Source: [Fair Work Ombudsman: Hours of work](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/employment-conditions/hours-of-work-breaks-and-rosters/hours-of-work), “About ordinary hours” and “Spread of hours”.

Verified: ordinary hours generally exclude overtime. Awards and enterprise agreements set ordinary-hour limits and their spread; these can differ by employment arrangement. Work outside the ordinary spread can attract overtime.

Limit: no universal 40-hour week, five-day roster, or fixed annual working-day count follows from this guidance. Use the actual roster and applicable instrument.

## E06 — Part-time and casual distinctions

Source: [Fair Work Ombudsman: Part-time employees](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/starting-employment/types-of-employees/part-time-employees), “What part-time employees get” and comparison with casual employment.

Verified: part-time leave entitlements generally scale with ordinary hours, but paid family and domestic violence leave is not pro-rated. Casual employment involves no firm advance commitment and a casual loading or specified casual rate.

Limit: do not pro-rate every entitlement or assume a universal loading percentage. The contract and applicable rules determine the actual values.

## E07 — Annual leave

Source: [Fair Work Ombudsman: Annual leave](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/leave/annual-leave), “How much annual leave” and “How annual leave accumulates”.

Verified: full-time and part-time employees receive four weeks based on ordinary hours under the described national-system rules. Additional shiftworker entitlement depends on the award/agreement definition. Annual leave accumulates, rolls over, and does not accrue during the listed unpaid-leave periods.

Limit: retain hours, accrual basis, paid/unpaid status, and instrument. Do not convert every week into five identical days or add paid leave to salary a second time.

## E08 — Personal/carer's leave

Source: [Fair Work Ombudsman: Paid sick and carer's leave](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/leave/sick-and-carers-leave/paid-sick-and-carers-leave), “How much paid sick and carer's leave”.

Verified: sick and carer's leave share one entitlement. The page expresses annual entitlement in ordinary hours, including the 1/26 calculation, and explains that spreading the same ordinary hours over different numbers of days does not change accrual.

Limit: entitlement, expected use, and cash value are different inputs. Do not assume all accrued leave will be used or paid out.

## E09 — Long service leave

Source: [Fair Work Ombudsman: Long service leave](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/leave/long-service-leave), “About”, “Pre-modern awards”, “Enterprise agreements”, and “Portable long service leave”.

Verified: state/territory laws determine many entitlements, with relevant award/agreement exceptions. Portable schemes exist for specified industries, and some long-serving casuals can qualify.

Limit: there is no single national accrual, vesting, or portability formula suitable for every package. Identify the scheme, service dates, and governing source before valuing it.

## E10 — Public holidays

Source: [Fair Work Ombudsman: Public holidays](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/employment-conditions/public-holidays), “Working outside your state or region” and “Substituting a public holiday”.

Verified: the work base determines public-holiday entitlement in the described example, rather than the worker's temporary location that day. Substitution arrangements can apply.

Limit: use a verified jurisdiction/year calendar, the actual roster, and substitutions. Do not deduct a fixed national number of public holidays from every package.

## E11 — Allowances

Source: [Fair Work Ombudsman: Allowances](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/pay-and-wages/penalty-rates-allowances-and-other-payments/allowances), “Calculating allowances” and “Workplace arrangements affecting allowances”.

Verified: applicable allowances depend on the award or agreement; annualised pay and contractual arrangements can affect how they are paid.

Limit: identify each allowance's basis, eligibility, inclusion in quoted pay, and actual amount. Tax and super treatment require separate verification.

## E12 — Penalties

Source: [Fair Work Ombudsman: Penalty rates](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/pay-and-wages/penalty-rates-allowances-and-other-payments/penalty-rates), “Calculating penalty rates”.

Verified: penalty rates depend on the applicable award or agreement. Salary and other arrangements can affect payment treatment.

Limit: do not invent a universal weekend multiplier or automatically stack penalties, overtime, and casual loading. Check the relevant instrument.

## E13 — Piece rates and commission

Source: [Fair Work Ombudsman: Piece rates and commission payments](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/pay-and-wages/minimum-wages/piece-rates-and-commission-payments), “Piece rates” and “Commission payments”.

Verified: piece rates pay for output and can coexist with hourly shifts. Commission can supplement pay or comprise the wage where permitted; awards/agreements impose relevant conditions.

Limit: output volume, sales, commission tiers, guarantee/draw, and expected attainment must be supplied. Do not treat a target commission as guaranteed salary.

## E14 — Training classifications

Source: [Fair Work Ombudsman: Apprentice and trainee pay rates](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/pay-and-wages/minimum-wages/apprentice-and-trainee-pay-rates), “Apprentice pay” and progression.

Verified: apprentice/trainee arrangements require a formal training contract; pay can depend on training progress, time, competency, and the applicable instrument. Being inexperienced alone does not establish an apprentice classification.

Limit: retrieve the specific classification and dated rate table before any automatic minimum-pay calculation.

## E15 — State-system example

Source: [Western Australian Government: Full time, part time and fixed term employment](https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/private-sector-labour-relations/full-time-part-time-and-fixed-term-employment). Page updated **3 September 2026**.

Verified: WA state-system employees have relevant state minimum conditions and WA awards. Fixed-term arrangements can be full-time or part-time.

Limit: fixed-term duration and hours classification are separate attributes. This page does not establish that every WA worker belongs to the state system.

## E16 — Contractor costs and responsibilities

Source: [business.gov.au: Contractor responsibilities](https://business.gov.au/people/contractors/contractor-responsibilities), “Insurance”, “Licensing”, and “Superannuation”.

Verified: contractor expenses can include income protection, liability and asset/revenue insurance, licences, and qualifications. Some contractors are employees for super purposes and require hirer contributions.

Limit: an ABN or contractor label does not settle super liability. Policy costs, excesses, cover, and exclusions need actual evidence; use the ATO research for classification.

## E17 — Contractor and regulated-worker distinctions

Source: [Fair Work Ombudsman: Contractor entitlements and support](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/find-help-for/independent-contractors/contractor-entitlements-and-support), “Entitlements” and “Minimum standards for some contractors”.

Verified: ordinary contractor arrangements do not carry the same employee entitlements, such as paid leave. Special minimum standards can apply to employee-like digital-platform workers and regulated road-transport contractors.

Limit: neither “all contractors receive employee leave” nor “no contractor has minimum standards” is a valid universal rule.

## E18 — Exit payments

Source: [Fair Work Ombudsman: Final pay](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/ending-employment/final-pay), “Employee's final pay”, “Sick and carer's leave”, and “Annual leave entitlements”.

Verified: final pay can include outstanding wages, annual leave/loading, and applicable notice, redundancy, or long-service amounts. The guidance distinguishes unused annual leave payout from personal/carer's leave, which is not paid out on ending employment.

Limit: distinguish an accrued existing asset and a one-time exit payment from recurring annual package value; verify actual conditions and tax treatment.

## E19 — Flexibility

Source: [Fair Work Ombudsman: Flexible working arrangements](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/employment-conditions/flexibility-in-the-workplace/flexible-working-arrangements), “Summary” and eligibility sections.

Verified: flexibility can concern hours, patterns, and location. Statutory request rights have eligibility and process conditions.

Limit: a right to request flexibility is not a guaranteed remote-work term. Record the actual agreement, review date, and any employer change provisions.

## E20 — Vehicle cost categories

Source: [Western Australian Government: Is an EV right for you?](https://www.wa.gov.au/service/environment/environment-information-services/ev-right-you), cost-comparison discussion. Page updated **7 October 2025**; its numerical lease examples use **June 2024** information.

Verified: the cost categories discussed include finance, maintenance, insurance, registration, tyres, and fuel/electricity. Costs vary with circumstances and provider terms.

Limit: the historical sample prices are expressly excluded from sal's current factual inputs. Use individual quotes/measurements; distinguish cash repayments from depreciation-based ownership valuation.

## E21 — Fuel and electricity units

Source: [Australian Government Green Vehicle Guide: How to guide](https://greenvehicleguide.gov.au/pages/ToolsAndCalculators/HowToGuide), “Information provided”, fuel-cost, and home-charging sections.

Verified: published fuel consumption uses L/100 km and electric consumption Wh/km, based on laboratory tests; actual consumption can differ. Its calculators use distance and fuel price, and electricity tariffs from the bill.

Limit: do not adopt the site's default annual kilometres or historic average fuel price as a user's commute. Record test-cycle or measured provenance and distinguish energy at the battery from energy billed.

## E22 — Household income definitions

Source: [Services Australia: How we use adjusted taxable income](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/how-we-use-adjusted-taxable-income?context=41186), “Family assistance payments” and “Child support”.

Verified: family assistance considers components beyond taxable income, including reportable benefits and super contributions; child-support treatment and relevant income periods differ. The page identifies Family Tax Benefit, Child Care Subsidy, and Parental Leave Pay as affected programs.

Limit: reduced taxable salary does not by itself establish increased household assistance. Full eligibility, rates, partner data, and reconciliation rules remain separate rule sets.

## E23 — Childcare costs

Source: [Services Australia: How much Child Care Subsidy you can get](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/how-much-child-care-subsidy-you-can-get?context=41186), calculation factors.

Verified: CCS depends on family income, hourly cap, service type, child age, family circumstances/recognised participation, and number of children. Subsidised hours apply per child.

Limit: no CCS percentage or cap is fixed here. Model actual sessions and fees separately from the subsidy; a daily-fee-only estimate cannot establish the full entitlement.

## E24 — Notice and termination conditions

Source: [Fair Work Ombudsman: Dismissal](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/ending-employment/dismissal), “Giving an employee notice of termination”.

Verified: required notice can be worked or paid instead, subject to exceptions, and not every employee receives NES notice.

Limit: tenure, termination reason, contract, and applicable instrument are required before pricing an exit scenario. A possible redundancy payment is not guaranteed recurring income.

## Evidence boundaries

- No actual offer, award classification, employment contract, travel route, fuel quote, insurance policy, household assessment, or private financial record was supplied or validated.
- Dates, amounts, and behavioural probabilities for those inputs must remain unset until supported; zero is not a substitute for unknown.
- These references verify the dependencies above. They do not certify a universal employment-law engine, award compliance, or a user's eligibility.
